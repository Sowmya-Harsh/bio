Hi, I'm Sowmya.

Data scientist · graph nerd · pipeline builder · relentless debugger

Hi! I'm Sowmya, a data scientist and machine learning engineer who likes turning messy, complicated data into tools people can actually use. I'm finishing a Master's in Data Science at the University of Luxembourg, where I focus on statistics, machine learning, and the kind of AI systems that have to work end to end — from the data pipeline all the way to the thing someone clicks on.

Right now I'm doing my thesis at the Luxembourg Centre for Systems Biomedicine, in the Bioinformatics Core. I've been untangling gene and protein identifiers across BridgeDb, HGNC, and a pile of other biomedical resources — more than a million mappings — and giving them a home in a graph-aware Neo4j schema with trust scores baked in. On top of that I built a full-stack Python platform so people can ingest, search, and actually see the graph instead of squinting at a spreadsheet.

Before the thesis, I spent a summer at the University of Luxembourg (with LIST) building an OCR-to-RAG pipeline that turned scanned workbooks into a searchable knowledge base. I used GPT-4o Vision to pull structured exercises, tables, and audio prompts off the page, wired up a multi-agent LLM pipeline for explainable retrieval, and wrapped the whole thing in a Streamlit interface with versioned runs.

And before Luxembourg, I was a Solution Developer at Brane Enterprises in Hyderabad, where I shipped 15+ end-to-end tools — data workflows, logic, front-ends — and learned how to move fast without breaking the people who depend on your software.

When I'm not on a deadline, I'm usually building something with graphs or language: PlayinGrocery, a multi-agent app that scrapes live grocery prices and optimizes a shopping list to a budget; a resume-to-job-fit analyzer built on Sentence-BERT; a biomedical literature miner that turns abstracts into knowledge graphs; and a GraphSAGE model that treats patients as a network to predict disease progression. I like problems where the structure of the data is half the answer.

Before all of this, I studied Civil Engineering at JNTU Kakinada in India — which sounds like a detour, but it's where I fell for mathematics and the habit of building things that have to hold up in the real world. That love stuck: I still tutor secondary-school students (S3–S7) in maths, and there's nothing like explaining a proof to a teenager to keep your own fundamentals honest.
